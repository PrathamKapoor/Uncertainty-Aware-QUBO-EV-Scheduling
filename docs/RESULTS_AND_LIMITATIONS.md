# Results and limitations

The output `artifacts/revised_v2/public_duration_stress.json.gz` is generated by `python -m experiments.public_dataset data/ChargingRecords.csv`. Among 22,083 eligible evaluation sessions, the synthetic service completion rate is 85.01% for F0, F1 and the 80% buffer, 93.56% for the 90% buffer, and 0% for the 95% buffer, whose 18-minute margin leaves no full 15-minute charging slot. The 90% minus F0 difference is 8.55 percentage points; its paired day-block 95% percentile interval is 8.20–8.91 points across 92 days. This interval does not remove repeated-user or repeated-charger dependence across days.

Mean simulated unmet energy is 0.0375 kWh for F0/F1 and 0.0161 kWh for the 90% buffer; the 95% buffer leaves 0.25 kWh unmet. Mean realized simulated energy cost is 0.1063 and 0.2339 arbitrary currency units for F0 and the 90% buffer, respectively. These are scenario-dependent simulated delivery costs, not observed bills or a controlled operational counterfactual.

The four-qubit F0 QAOA run has exact optimum 0.125, p=1, 256 final shots, and 20 COBYLA evaluations at most. Optimal bitstrings appeared with probability 0.2383; physically feasible schedules appeared with probability 0.8672; uniform random values are 0.0625 and 0.75 respectively. The best sampled gap was zero, but the sampled mean energy was 3.1113. A single seed and low optimizer budget do not establish reliable QAOA superiority. The old held-out ACN results use different, defective definitions and are not directly comparable.

The 90% buffer QUBO also uses four qubits. Its exact optimum is 0.25 and the best sampled gap was zero. Optimal bitstrings appeared with probability 0.3672; 0.9297 of shots obeyed the buffered window and 0.5664 both obeyed the window and met the modeled service target. This is one seed, not a general comparison.
