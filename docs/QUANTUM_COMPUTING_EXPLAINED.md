# Quantum computation in the synthetic pilot

A qubit represents each binary variable, including the two capacity slack bits. Hadamard gates prepare a superposition of all six-bit assignments. The cost Hamiltonian applies phases according to the Ising energy; mixer rotations change amplitudes between assignments. At depth `p=1`, the circuit applies one cost evolution and one mixer layer. The classical COBYLA optimizer chooses the two angles using sampled energies from Qiskit Aer, then the circuit is sampled again. It does not independently solve the scheduling problem.

`quantum.qaoa._build_qaoa_ansatz` implements the circuit; `_expectation_from_counts` and `run_qaoa` interpret Qiskit's bit order. `artifacts/revised_v2/circuit_p1.txt` and `circuit_p2.txt` are drawn from those circuits. The transpiled pilot uses 6 qubits, depth 22 and 16 CX gates at p=1, and depth 37 and 32 CX gates at p=2. Aer is a classical simulator, not quantum hardware. The p=1, seed 0 run sampled an optimal QUBO state 38 times and a physically feasible schedule 68 times in 256 shots. These probabilities are different because feasible schedules can have suboptimal slack bits or cost. Two seeds at each depth are exploratory and too few to establish a general performance difference. This small experiment does not demonstrate a speedup or quantum advantage; exact enumeration provides the reference.

The Ising identity offset does not affect circuit phases relative to one another, but must be added back when comparing numerical energies. The revised exhaustive test verifies the offset as well as the operator's basis ordering.
